Alibaba Cloud AI Hackathon Pakistan 2026: Grand Finale, Winners & Highlights

The Alibaba Cloud AI Hackathon Pakistan 2026 brought young developers and university students together to build AI-powered products for practical challenges. The Islamabad finale showcased projects across six innovation areas.

The national grand finale took place on October 24, 2026, at the Pak-China Friendship Centre in Islamabad. The competition attracted more than 16,000 applications nationwide, with 749 finalists presenting 300 AI projects after progressing through regional rounds.

Organised through a collaboration involving Alibaba Cloud, Alkhidmat Foundation Pakistan's Bano Qabil initiative, and Cognix Solutions, the event focused on developing practical artificial intelligence solutions for real-world challenges.

A Pakistan AI hackathon for young innovators

The Alibaba Cloud AI Hackathon was designed to give Pakistani students and developers an opportunity to turn ideas into working AI-powered products.

Instead of focusing only on theoretical concepts or presentations, participants were encouraged to develop solutions that could address problems in different sectors.

The programme included technical training, mentorship, development tools, and multiple evaluation stages.

Its nationwide participation demonstrated the growing interest of young Pakistanis in artificial intelligence and emerging technologies.

From Regional Rounds to the Islamabad Grand Finale

Before reaching the national finale, participating teams went through regional evaluation rounds in three major cities.

The regional events were held on:

  • Karachi: October 3, 2026
  • Islamabad: October 11, 2026
  • Lahore: October 17, 2026

The selected finalists then gathered in Islamabad on October 24 for the national competition.

The grand finale brought together hundreds of participants to present their projects and demonstrate how AI could be applied to practical challenges.

300 AI Projects Across Six Innovation Categories

One of the most interesting aspects of the competition was the variety of problems participants attempted to solve.

The hackathon focused on six major areas:

1. Smart Agriculture

AI-based ideas focused on supporting agriculture, improving farming practices, and addressing challenges in food production.

2. Healthcare

Participants explored technology-based solutions that could support healthcare services, patient care, and medical accessibility.

3. Education

Projects focused on improving learning experiences, educational accessibility, tutoring, and assessment through AI.

4. Financial Inclusion

This category encouraged ideas that could make financial services more accessible through digital technology.

5. Urdu and Regional Language Technology

A particularly relevant category for Pakistan, encouraging AI solutions for Urdu and regional languages such as Sindhi, Punjabi, and Pashto.

6. Open Innovation

This category allowed participants to explore other AI-powered ideas that did not fit into the remaining categories.

The variety of these categories showed how artificial intelligence can be applied to challenges beyond traditional software development.

FAST-NUCES Team Salix Wins the Grand Champion Title

One of the major highlights following the national finale was the achievement of Team Salix from FAST-NUCES Islamabad.

The team secured the Grand Champion title at the national finale.

FAST-NUCES teams also received recognition in several other categories. These included:

  • Kisaan Rahbar from FAST-NUCES Islamabad, which won the Smart Agriculture track.
  • AI in Governance from FAST-NUCES Multan, which finished as runner-up in Open Innovation.
  • MediNova AI from FAST-NUCES Karachi, which was runner-up in Healthcare.
  • PakAssist from FAST-NUCES Karachi, which was runner-up in Urdu and Regional Language Technology.
  • Muhafiz AI from FAST-NUCES Karachi, which secured second runner-up overall.

These achievements highlighted the participation and innovation coming from multiple university campuses.

MUET Students Receive a Special Accessibility Award

Another notable achievement came from Mehran University of Engineering and Technology (MUET), Jamshoro.

The university's students received the Special Award in Accessibility for their project, Saans.

Saans is an English and Urdu offline-first web application designed to help frontline healthcare workers screen children under five for tuberculosis using relevant screening guidelines.

The project demonstrated how student-developed technology can focus on accessibility and practical healthcare challenges.

What This Hackathon Means for Pakistan's Tech Community

Beyond the competition, the event gave young innovators a place to develop practical skills, present working projects, receive feedback, and connect with the wider technology community.

The focus on healthcare, education, agriculture, financial inclusion, and regional languages also demonstrated the importance of developing AI solutions that respond to local needs.

For students, these AI projects show how ideas can move beyond classroom learning toward practical products.

Final Thoughts

The national finale highlighted the growing potential of Pakistan's young technology community.

With more than 16,000 applications, 749 finalists, and 300 AI projects, the national finale represented an important moment for student innovation and artificial intelligence in Pakistan.

From FAST-NUCES Team Salix winning the Grand Champion title to MUET's accessibility-focused Saans project receiving special recognition, the competition showcased different ways young innovators are approaching real-world challenges.

As artificial intelligence continues to develop, platforms that encourage students to build, experiment, and collaborate can play an important role in shaping Pakistan's technology future.
